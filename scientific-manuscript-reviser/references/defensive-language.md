# Functional defensiveness audit

Calibrated claims without anticipatory self-downgrading. Evidence supports claim
strength in both directions. Scientific coherence and defensiveness are separate
passes but must share evidence about strength, uncertainty and boundaries.

For each relevant sentence record source location, its function, the corresponding
claim/evidence/uncertainty and whether its removal would alter scientific meaning.
Classify it as one of

- necessary calibration
- optional hedge
- redundant defensive language
- claim weakening without evidence basis

Genuine uncertainty, limited evaluation scope, a known failure and justified
statistical caution are necessary. Preserve them even when a phrase appears in
a warning lexicon. A phrase alone does not decide its scientific function.

“We acknowledge”, “we do not claim”, “interpret cautiously”, “although limited”,
“nevertheless” and “despite these limitations” are context cues, not banned words.
Inspect consecutive hedge stacks, imagined reviewer objections, introductory
apologies and a results section that repeatedly weakens every finding.

Unsupported hedges and weakening a well-supported result deserve suggestions.
Consolidating repetitive caution may help, but preserve the underlying uncertainty
and its visibility. Removing a real limitation fails invariance. Adding confidence
without evidence fails too. If a scientific claim itself needs strengthening or
calibration, classify the suggestion as high risk and seek author resolution.
Do not auto-apply scientific corrections under the label of language editing.

The structured quality check includes calibrated_claim, and the semantic audit
includes no_unsupported_weakening. Neither can be established by regex. For a
fluent but unsupported mechanism, scientific repair paths lead the bank before
cosmetic improvements.
